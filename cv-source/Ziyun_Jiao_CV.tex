\resumeSubheadingalter
{Resource-Aware Benchmarking of Classical and Deep Time-Series Forecasters}
{National University of Singapore, course research project}{Jan. -- May 2025}
\resumeItemListStart
\resumeItem{Study design: }
{Benchmarked six forecasters (Prophet, LightGBM, DLinear, LSTM, Transformer, DARNN)
on five univariate series drawn from energy consumption, air quality, manufacturing
productivity, urban traffic and biomedical gait, each with a dataset-specific
preprocessing pipeline covering imputation, winsorisation, calendar and event
features, and train-set-only scaling to prevent leakage;}
\resumeItem{Evaluation beyond error: }
{Scored every model and dataset pair on four axes: forecast error (MAE, RMSE, MAPE,
sMAPE), decision-relevant accuracy (directional and threshold accuracy), deployment
cost (training time, peak memory, early stopping), and noise robustness ($\Delta$MAE
under additive Gaussian perturbation, $\sigma = 0.05$);}
\resumeItem{Finding: }
{Peak memory varied by roughly three orders of magnitude across models, from tens of
gigabytes for the attention-based recurrent model to tens of megabytes for the linear
and gradient-boosting baselines, and the most accurate model on a given series was
frequently neither the cheapest nor the most noise-stable. LightGBM degraded by up to
63\% MAE under perturbation on the smallest series while the recurrent models stayed
within 1.2\%, and several deep models fell below 0.50 directional accuracy on that
series. The study concludes that forecaster selection should be posed against
deployment constraints rather than as error minimisation alone.}
\resumeItemListEnd
