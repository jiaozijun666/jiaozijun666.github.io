    National University of Singapore

  • 2025 - 2025
    Resource-aware benchmarking of classical and deep time-series forecasters
    National University of Singapore — course research project
    • Study design: benchmarked six forecasters (Prophet, LightGBM, DLinear, LSTM, Transformer, DARNN) on five univariate series drawn from energy consumption, air quality, manufacturing productivity, urban traffic and biomedical gait, each with a dataset-specific preprocessing pipeline covering imputation, winsorisation, calendar and event features, and train-set-only scaling to prevent leakage.
    • Evaluation beyond error: scored every model–dataset pair on forecast error (MAE, RMSE, MAPE, sMAPE), decision-relevant accuracy (directional and threshold accuracy), deployment cost (training time, peak memory, early stopping), and noise robustness (ΔMAE under additive Gaussian perturbation, σ = 0.05).
    • Finding: peak memory varied by roughly three orders of magnitude across models, and the most accurate model on a given series was frequently neither the cheapest nor the most noise-stable — LightGBM degraded by up to 63% MAE under perturbation on the smallest series while the recurrent models stayed within 1.2%. Forecaster selection should be posed against deployment constraints rather than as error minimisation alone.
  • 2023 - 2024

    University of Southampton — undergraduate dissertation, sole author, advised by Dr. Itamar Yaakov
    • Reviewed and compared neural-network quantum states, matrix-product and projected entangled-pair tensor networks, and restricted Boltzmann machines as compressed variational representations of exponentially large wavefunctions, covering variational optimisation, imaginary-time evolution, and variational Monte Carlo with stochastic reconfiguration.
    • Analysed the expressivity relationship between restricted Boltzmann machine and matrix-product-state representations and the conditions for conversion between them, using entanglement entropy as the comparison criterion.

Projects

  • 2025
    Multi-agent equity analysis with large language models

    Coursework project, National University of Singapore.

    • Co-designed the shared state object through which four analyst agents (fundamentals, technical, sentiment, adversarial) exchange information: agents never call one another; tools write into six typed domains and downstream agents read the fields they need, keeping each agent independently testable and replaceable.
    • Separated a system-level prompt fixing output format, tool-use protocol and fallback behaviour from role-specific prompts constraining each analyst’s methodology and risk boundaries.
    • Specified a five-part adversarial challenge applied to the analyst reports, testing for over-optimistic assumptions, under-weighted macro and governance risk, cross-report contradictions, assumption fragility and horizon-separated forecasts. The project delivered a working system; no backtesting was carried out.
  • 2025
    Scaled conjugate gradient methods for deep learning optimisation

    Coursework project, National University of Singapore.

    • The team studied a hybrid optimiser introducing a dynamic scaling factor into the conjugate-gradient update rule, with convergence rates argued at O(1/N) under a constant step size and O(1/√N) under a decaying step size, benchmarked against SGD, Adam and AdaBelief on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and IMDb.
    • Individual contribution: wrote the discussion and possible-extensions analysis, examining which assumptions in the convergence argument constrain applicability to non-convex settings. The derivation and the empirical work were carried out by other team members.
